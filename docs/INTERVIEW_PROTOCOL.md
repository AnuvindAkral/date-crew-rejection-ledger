# Matchmaker interview protocol

A ready-to-use guide for five conversations with TDC matchmakers, to be run only when access becomes available. This is a validation plan, not a research claim. It has not been executed.

**Status: prepared, unrun.** Do not present any output of this protocol as though the conversations happened. They have not.

---

## How to use this

- One matchmaker per conversation, 30 minutes each.
- Use a recent example wherever the question asks for one. A live, specific case is worth more than a general answer.
- Capture verbatim quotes, not paraphrases. Write down what the matchmaker said, then separately what you inferred.
- Do not pitch the acceptance debrief, the scorecard, or any feature before question 5. The point is to hear the current workflow and workarounds, not to get a reaction to a proposal.

## Capture format

For each question, record three columns:

| What they said (verbatim) | What they actually did (observed) | My interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| | | |

Keep observed behaviour separate from interpretation. A matchmaker saying "I remember everyone" is interpretation; a matchmaker opening a notebook to find a client's last reason is observed behaviour.

## The five conversations

### Question 1. When a client accepts someone, what do you learn that you didn't know before?

**Why this matters.** The whole debrief rests on whether acceptance carries information rejection data does not.

**Recent-example prompt.** "Walk me through the last time a client said yes to someone you didn't expect them to. What changed in your understanding of that client?"

**Probes.**
- Was it the acceptance itself, or something the client said about it, that taught you?
- Did it change what you searched for next, or only how you explained the next profile?

### Question 2. Where do you record that information, and does it influence the next recommendation?

**Why this matters.** This tells us whether the signal is already captured somewhere, and whether capture leads to action.

**Recent-example prompt.** "Think of the most useful thing you learned from a client's positive response this month. Where does it live now, and did it change a later recommendation?"

**Probes.**
- Is it in the CRM notes, your head, a message thread, or nowhere?
- If a colleague took over this client tomorrow, would they find it?

### Question 3. How do you know whether a disappointing profile came from poor sourcing, incomplete information, or a mismatch in expectations?

**Why this matters.** This is the scorecard gate: whether channel-level comparison is even meaningful to the people doing the work.

**Recent-example prompt.** "Tell me about a profile the client rejected that you were surprised they rejected. Where did it come from, and why do you think it missed?"

**Probes.**
- Do you notice some sources producing more misses than others, or is it usually about the client?
- What would you need to see to trust a per-channel view?

### Question 4. Which mistakes keep recurring despite the information already available to you?

**Why this matters.** This tests whether the problem is missing information or unused information. The debrief only helps the former.

**Recent-example prompt.** "What's the mistake you've caught yourself making more than once, even though you had the information to avoid it?"

**Probes.**
- Was the information recorded, or was it in your memory?
- Would a reminder at the moment you needed it have changed the outcome?

### Question 5. What would save you meaningful time or improve your next recommendation without adding another administrative step?

**Why this matters.** The debrief must reduce effort, not add a form. This is asked last so the matchmaker answers from their own work, not from our proposal.

**Prompt.** "If you could change one thing about how information flows to you right before you send a profile, what would it be? Assume nothing you add creates more typing."

**Probes.**
- What do you re-read or re-ask today that you shouldn't have to?
- What one piece of context, if it appeared automatically, would make the next send better?

## How findings map to the build decision

- If Question 1 and 2 show acceptance is informative and currently lost, the acceptance debrief is justified.
- If Question 2 shows the information is already captured but not consulted, the debrief is solving the wrong half, and the fix is surfacing, not capture.
- If Question 3 shows channel is not a meaningful axis, the sourcing scorecard is excluded, and this protocol documents why.
- If Question 4 shows mistakes come from unused information, the ledger's surfacing matters more than any new capture.
